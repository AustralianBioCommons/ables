## Project title

Biodiversity and microbiota: a novel pathway to allergy and asthma prevention

## Collaborators and funding

Research Centre for Hauora and Health, Massey University, NZ; HRC funded (23/086)

## Contact(s)

- Steven Taylor, Flinders University, <steven.taylor@flinders.edu.au>

## Project description and aims

AIM:
To relate the environmental microbiome (indoor and outdoor) to asthma and allergy risk in children at high risk.
To relate the human microbiome (stool, nasal, skin) microbiome to asthma and allergy risk in children at high risk.

IMPACT:
This may explain epidemiological associations linking particular urban environments to asthma and allergy, and identify microbial factors that can modify this effect.

Planned analyses:
Shotgun metagenomic sequencing will be performed on environmental samples (floor dust, doormats, soil) and host samples (skin swab, nasal swab, stool) from 150 children across 2 timepoints living across diverse settings (defined by level of surrounding biodiversity). microbiota characteristics will be compared between samples, within individuals, as well as between individuals and related to environmental biodiversity, demographics, living conditions (e.g. pets), and asthma and allergy.
